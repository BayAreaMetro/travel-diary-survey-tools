2 — Convergence & Fit

Population totals are derived from the first household- and person-level control. What to look for: • Convergence: Did the balancer converge within the specified tolerance and iteration limit? • Fit: How close are the weighted totals to the targets? Look at MAPE and the % error bars below. • Household/Person: Do both household and persons match? If not you may need to apply greater importance on h_total or p_total to overcome the inherent sampling bias against large-households that results in total population being deflated. • Effective Sample Size (ESS): How much variability in weights? If ESS is low, the balancer is relying heavily on a few records, which can lead to unstable estimates. • Coefficient of Variation (CV): Another measure of weight variability; high CV indicates extreme weights. Metric summary: • MAPE is the mean |% error| across all control categories for the zone. • ESS = (∑w)2 / ∑w2 (Kish); above 80% is good, below 50% suggests a few records dominate. • CV Coefficient of variation of weights: standard deviation / mean. High CV (e.g. above 0.5) indicates extreme weight variability, which can lead to unstable estimates.

3 — Weight Distribution

What to look for:
• Weights not too variable (high CV) and not too extreme (EF hitting bounds).
• We expect a roughly log-normal distribution of weights and not too lumpy; it is plotted on log-scale so it should be closer to “normal”.
• Multi-modality is not wrong per se, but is indicative that weights are being driven by a few key variables (e.g. a single dominant control category) and may be unstable.
